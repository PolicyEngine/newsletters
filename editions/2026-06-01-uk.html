Cancelling the fuel duty rise

We use policyengine.py to estimate the fiscal cost and household impact of cancelling the fuel-duty rise scheduled in the Autumn Budget 2025 — the staged unwinding of the 5p cut plus the April 2027 RPI uprating that together take duty to ~60p/L.

Key findings

• Holding duty flat at 52.95p/L instead of the planned 60.10p/L costs the Treasury £3.12bn in 2027-28, rising to £4.42bn by 2029-30
• The narrower 5p-only framing used in press coverage costs £2.18bn in 2027-28, since it excludes the April 2027 RPI uprating (IFS gives ~£2.3bn/yr)
• The de-facto freeze since 2011-12 has cost £124.74bn in foregone receipts versus an RPI-uprated counterfactual
• The saving skews to higher earners: the richest fifth of households gain ~£165/year against ~£50 for the poorest fifth. No-one loses

Universal Credit rebalancing analysis

The Universal Credit Act 2025 bundles two changes effective April 2026: an above-CPI uplift to the standard allowance, and a freeze of the monthly health element for new LCWRA claims. We quantify the fiscal and distributional impact across 2026-27 to 2029-30, benchmarked against the DWP Impact Assessment and IFS.

• The standard allowance uplift gains 5.99m households £1.67bn; the new-claimant health element freeze costs 2.06m households £2.33bn
• The two legs largely cancel at the package level (net −£0.66bn in 2029-30), but gainers and losers are different households
• The SA uplift is progressive — the poorest decile gains most — with a net −0.05pp change in the headline poverty rate (~39k fewer people in poverty)
