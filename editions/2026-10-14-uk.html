New Dashboard · Energy bills

Energy bills and the Autumn Budget 2026

Our new dashboard estimates what UK households will pay for gas and electricity in 2026-27 and 2027-28, and what seven Autumn Budget support options would cost and who they would reach.

What we find

•In 2027-28, households would pay £386 more a year on average than at April–June 2026 prices.
•In 2027-28, a £400 payment to every household would cost £11.8bn, freezing the price cap at April–June 2026 tariffs £11.4bn and a £300 Cost of Living Payment on means-tested benefits £2.4bn.

New Dashboard · Energy

Targeted energy bill discount

A proposed discount would take £175 off winter energy bills for households in Great Britain on a means-tested benefit or where no one has an income of £24,000 or more. Our new dashboard estimates its cost and reach, alongside four other designs.

What we find

•It costs £2.09bn in 2026-27 and reaches 11.95m households, at full take-up.
•Households in the bottom income decile gain £153 a year on average; those in the top decile £6.

New Dashboard · Childcare

Free childcare hours and a 75% subsidy

Explore the cost and household impact of two childcare reforms: 15 free hours a week for every child in England from 9 months (30 where parents work and earn under £100,000), and a UK-wide 75% subsidy replacing Tax-Free Childcare.

What we find

•Including parents’ labour supply responses, they cost £1.93bn and £4.17bn in 2027-28.
•Households with a child under 5 gain £2,099 a year on average.

New Research · AI

Who bears the AI shock?

If AI displaces a share of UK jobs, who bears the loss: workers, lower-income households or the public finances? Our new study runs AI-driven job losses, wage changes and higher returns to capital through the UK tax-benefit system. It compares five assumptions about which workers AI affects.

What we find

•If 7% of employees lost their jobs for a year, the Exchequer cost would be £18.2bn and absolute poverty would rise by 1.85pp.
•The same loss through wage cuts would cost £26.6bn but raise absolute poverty by 0.10pp.
