Briefing · 22 November

The Milburn review: our briefing on its proposals for young people’s benefits

The review proposes ending the Universal Credit (UC) health element and the Personal Independence Payment (PIP) for young people. We estimate the effect of ending the two from April 2029, as a static estimate: before any replacement support and with no change in employment. Our dashboard models five of the review’s proposals, by year, by age and for illustrative households.

What we find: ending the UC health element and PIP together

•Ending the UC health element and PIP for young people would cut spending by £4.7 billion in 2029-30, or £2.8 billion if existing claimants moved only when reassessed.
•580,000 people aged 16 to 24 (7.8%) would live in households whose income falls.
•19.4% of young people we estimate are NEET (not in education, employment or training) would see household income fall, against 5.8% of other 16 to 24-year-olds.
•342,000 more people of all ages would be in absolute poverty before housing costs (+0.5 percentage points).

Energy bills · 19 November

What a January 2027 price cap rise would mean for household energy bills

The consultancy Cornwall Insight forecasts that the energy price cap will rise by 26% in January, to £2,166 a year for a household with typical use. We estimate what that would mean for households at different incomes.

What we find

•The average bill in Great Britain would rise by £415 a year: £367 in the lowest-income tenth, £522 in the highest.
•The lowest-income tenth would spend 11.5% of income on energy, up from 9.3%.
•3.6 million households would spend over a tenth of their income on energy, up from 2.2 million.
