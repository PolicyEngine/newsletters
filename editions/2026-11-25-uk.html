Briefing · 22 November

The Milburn review: our briefing on its proposals for young people’s benefits

The review proposes ending the Universal Credit (UC) health element and the Personal Independence Payment (PIP) for young people. We estimate the effect of ending both from April 2029, before any of the replacement support the review proposes.

What we find

•Ending both would save the government £4.7 billion in 2029-30. If people already claiming kept them until they were reassessed, the saving that year would be £2.8 billion.
•580,000 people aged 16 to 24, or 7.8% of that age group, would live in households whose income falls.
•Young people not in education, employment or training (NEET) would be most affected: 19.4% would live in households whose income falls, against 5.8% of other 16 to 24-year-olds.
•342,000 more people would be in absolute poverty before housing costs, a rise of 0.5 percentage points.

Energy bills · 19 November

What a January 2027 price cap rise would mean for household energy bills

The consultancy Cornwall Insight forecasts that the energy price cap will rise by 26% in January, to £2,166 a year for a household with typical use. We estimate what that would mean for households at different incomes.

What we find

•The average household would spend £415 a year more on electricity and gas, £2,117 in total.
•The lowest-income tenth of households would spend 11.5% of their income on energy, up from 9.3%. For the highest-income tenth, the share would rise from 1.6% to 2.0%.
•3.6 million households would spend more than a tenth of their income on energy, up from 2.2 million.

Ofgem will announce the actual January cap, which may differ from the forecast.
