Briefing · 22 November

The Milburn review and young people’s benefits

The review proposes ending the Universal Credit (UC) health element for under-25s and replacing the Personal Independence Payment (PIP) for young people with three new forms of support. We estimate ending the two from April 2029, without the replacement support, the Youth Allowance, the work allowance or the rise in employment the review expects. Our dashboard models five of its proposals.

What we find (static estimates)

•Ending the UC health element and PIP for young people would save £4.4 billion in 2029-30, or £2.6 billion if existing claimants moved only when reassessed.
•609,000 people aged 16 to 24 (8.1%) would live in households whose income falls.
•21.0% of young people we estimate are NEET (not in education, employment or training) would see household income fall, against 6.2% of other 16 to 24-year-olds.
•291,000 more people of all ages would be in absolute poverty before housing costs (+0.4 percentage points).

The average is over everyone aged 16 to 24, most of whom see no change.

Energy bills · 25 November

Ofgem’s January energy price cap

On 25 November Ofgem announced that the energy price cap will rise by 26% in January, to £2,166 a year for a household with typical use, the level Cornwall Insight forecast. We estimate what it means for households at different incomes, holding energy use fixed.

What we find

•The average bill in Great Britain would rise by £415 a year: £374 in the lowest-income tenth, £523 in the highest.
•The lowest-income tenth would spend 11.6% of income on energy, up from 9.4%.
•3.6 million households would spend over a tenth of their income on energy at January rates for a full year, up from 2.3 million.
