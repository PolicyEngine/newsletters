Briefing · 22 November

The Milburn review: our briefing on its proposals for young people’s benefits

The review proposes ending the Universal Credit health element and PIP for young people. We estimate the two removals from April 2029, before any replacement support.

What we find

•The two removals would save £4.7 billion in 2029-30. If existing claimants moved over only as they are reassessed, the saving that year would be £2.8 billion.
•580,000 people aged 16 to 24 (7.8% of that age group) would live in households whose net income falls.
•Absolute poverty before housing costs would rise by 0.5 percentage points, or 342,000 people.

Energy bills · 19 November

What a January 2027 price cap rise would mean for household energy bills

Cornwall Insight forecasts a 26% rise in the price cap in January, to £2,166 a year for a typical household. Here is what that would mean across incomes.

What we find

•Average household spending on electricity and gas would rise by £415 a year, to £2,117.
•In the lowest income decile, energy spending would rise from 9.3% to 11.5% of net income; in the highest, from 1.6% to 2.0%.
•The number of households spending more than 10% of their net income on energy would rise from 2.2 million to 3.6 million.
