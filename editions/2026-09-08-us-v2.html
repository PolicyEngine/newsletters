Why join

The Census Bureau publishes Income, Poverty and Health Insurance Coverage in the United States: 2025 at 10am ET on September 15. Four hours later, we go live. Our guests give a few minutes each on what the new numbers say. Then we turn to what state reforms would do to child poverty: David walks through the Child Poverty Impact Dashboard live, building a reform, tracing one family’s taxes and benefits across the earnings range, and comparing statewide and district impacts.

Configure a reform, a state Child Tax Credit or EITC, a dependent exemption, a child allowance, a SNAP expansion, or a federal proposal such as the American Family Act, and see its impact on child poverty, state and federal budgets, income distribution, congressional districts, and individual households, for all 50 states and DC. Every estimate comes from PolicyEngine’s open-source microsimulation model over calibrated state and local-area microdata, and every result has a shareable link. The dashboard opens to the public on Tuesday, September 15.

The dashboard goes public the morning of the 15th. Researchers, advocates, and legislative staff will leave knowing how to score a reform in their own state.

PolicyBench

39 models scored, and an expansion page for new regions and programs

PolicyBench tests how accurately language models calculate household taxes and benefits: the same 100 real households and 18 outputs for every model, each queried under the request configuration its model card records. The September 5 board scores 39 models, up from 26 in late July. GPT-5.6 Sol leads with an exact-match score of 89.2%, where dollar answers within $1 count as exact. GPT-6 Astra, released September 4, debuted second at 88.0%, with Claude Fable 5.1 third at 86.9% and Kimi K3 fourth. Other arrivals since July include Gemini 3.8 Flash, GLM-5.3 Flash, Grok 4.6, and Inkling.

The leaderboard carries a serving-sensitivity note. The board forces the answer tool call for models whose card selects the tool contract, which switches off extended thinking for three Claude rows; Claude Fable 5.1 rejects forced calls, answers as JSON, and reasons either way. Re-runs shown beside the board score Claude Fable 5.1 at 88.2% under tool choice auto (a change of response transport) and Claude Fable 5 at 87.5% under tool choice auto with the whole household in one request (two changes at once). The board itself is unchanged, and the next board version moves every model to auto.

New: policybench.org/expand, for anyone who wants PolicyBench run on their region, population, or program.

In the model

Nine state premium assistance programs joined the model in August: Connecticut, Vermont, New Jersey, Massachusetts, Washington, New Mexico, Colorado, California, and Maryland.

The model also carries the first OBBBA implementation rules: the SNAP work requirement now covers adults aged 60 to 64, with the 30-hour work exemption and county-level waivers, and Medicaid community engagement rules include the hardship exceptions for hospitalization and long-distance care. That is 127 policyengine-us releases since our July newsletter.

PolicyEngine in others’ work

Brookings. Sarah Calame and Tara Watson’s Supporting children and incentivizing employment: the future of the Earned Income Tax Credit and the Child Tax Credit (July 2026) used PolicyEngine to estimate the distributional effects, poverty effects, and cost of the reform proposals it evaluates, on a 2025 post-OBBBA baseline.

Aspen Institute. Bridging the information gap: how organizations can assess and use benefits screeners (July 2026) identifies PolicyEngine and Benefits Kitchen as the only nationally comprehensive policy rules engines built for benefits screening that offer external APIs for multi-program eligibility.

Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget. New approaches to Social Security benefit taxation (July 30) draws on estimates modeled in PolicyEngine and links to our interactive dashboard on the taxation of benefits.

Retirement Figures. The retirement-planning tool validates its tax engine against PolicyEngine US, and reports that its federal calculations match to within one dollar in every scenario it tests.

TaxVant. A new tax-planning tool for do-it-yourself filers, TaxVant is built on PolicyEngine’s open-source engine, which its founder describes as the first comprehensive open-source tax engine covering federal and all 50 states.
