New Dashboard · Cost of living

The Middle East war and UK living standards

Our new dashboard lets you explore how higher energy, fuel and food prices from the Middle East war would affect UK households in 2027-28 under three price scenarios, and compare ten policy responses.

What we find

•At summer 2026 prices, households would pay £538 more a year on average.
•Households in the bottom income quintile would lose 1.65% of income, against 0.60% for the top quintile.

New Dashboard · Energy

Targeted energy bill discount

The Resolution Foundation has proposed a £175 discount on winter energy bills for households in Great Britain where someone receives a means-tested benefit or where no one has taxable income of £24,000 or more. Our new dashboard estimates the cost, reach and distributional effects of this and four alternative designs.

What we find

•It costs £2.09bn in 2026-27 and reaches 11.95m households, at full take-up.
•Households in the bottom income decile gain £153 a year on average; those in the top decile £6.

New Dashboard · Childcare

Free childcare hours and a 75% subsidy

Explore the cost and household impact of two childcare reforms: 15 free hours a week for every child in England from 9 months (30 where parents work and earn under £100,000), and a UK-wide 75% subsidy replacing Tax-Free Childcare.

What we find

•Before behavioural responses, they cost £1.97bn and £4.84bn in 2027-28.
•Households with a child under 5 gain £2,099 a year on average.

Peer reviewed · Journal of Open Source Software

policyengine: a microsimulation tool for tax-benefit policy analysis

Our paper on policyengine, the open-source Python package behind every analysis in this edition, has been published in the Journal of Open Source Software after peer review. The package calculates UK and US taxes and benefits for a single household or the whole population, under current law or a reform.

Ahmadi, Ghenis, Woodruff, Makarchuk and Volk (2026). Journal of Open Source Software, 11(125), 11115. doi:10.21105/joss.11115

New Research · AI

Who bears the AI shock?

Our new study traces how AI-driven job losses, wage changes and higher returns to capital would pass through the UK tax-benefit system, and who would bear the cost.

What we find

•If 7% of employees lost their jobs for a year, the Exchequer cost would be £18.2bn and absolute poverty would rise by 1.85pp.
•The same loss through wage cuts would cost £26.6bn but raise absolute poverty by 0.10pp.
