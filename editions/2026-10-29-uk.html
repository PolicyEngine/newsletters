Autumn Budget 2026: our briefing on what it means for households

What the seven household measures we model do together and one by one, how our estimates compare with the official costings, and which of the Budget’s measures we don’t yet model.

What we find

•The seven measures cost £3.11 billion in 2026-27. From 2028-29 to 2031-32 they raise money, as the higher rates on property income raise more than the other measures cost.
•In 2026-27 they raise household net income by £107 a year on average, and every income decile gains between £98 and £110.
•The highest-income decile loses £50 a year on average in 2028-29, rising to £56 in 2031-32, as property income tax rates rise.
